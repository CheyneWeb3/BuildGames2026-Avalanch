## Product Vision

The Haus is being built as a modular cashier, balance, and spend layer for Avalanche applications, games, social platforms, and community tools.

Long term, the goal is to give builders reusable infrastructure for deposits, balances, spending, rewards, settlements, and withdrawals without forcing users through a full wallet transaction every time they want to interact. Instead of every app building its own payment and balance system from scratch, The Haus is designed as a shared layer that different products can plug into.

A big part of the vision is flexibility in how users access and use the system. The MVP already demonstrates multiple interaction and auth paths including wallet-based access, SIWE-style flows, Telegram-linked interactions, and Google sign-in based spend flows. That matters because real products do not all onboard users the same way, and The Haus is being designed to support that wider reality.

Over time, The Haus can grow into infrastructure that powers browser apps, spend-enabled user accounts, game and reward systems, social and community tools, linked identity flows, and third-party product integrations across Avalanche. The long-term direction is not one standalone demo, but a reusable cashier rail that makes Avalanche products easier to use, easier to extend, and better suited for real user activity.

## User acquisition strategy

Our first users are Avalanche-native builders, app users, game users, and community participants who already understand wallets but want smoother product interaction than direct wallet prompts for every repeated action.

The first acquisition channel is product-led exposure through the live MVP. The goal is to show working spend and interaction flows in a real environment so users can immediately understand the benefit of the cashier model. Instead of explaining the system only at a technical level, we want users to feel the difference through actual use.

The second channel is community-based distribution through Telegram and related crypto-native spaces. Since The Haus already supports linked social interaction paths, it has a natural advantage in reaching users where they already spend time and then moving them into broader app usage.

The third channel is direct builder and ecosystem outreach. The Haus is not only for end users. It is also infrastructure for teams that want a reusable cashier and spend layer instead of building their own from scratch. That makes developer and partner adoption part of the GTM strategy from the beginning.

Our early conversion strategy is to get users into one clear funded and interactive flow, prove that it is smoother than wallet-only interaction, and then expand them into more linked account usage, more modules, and repeat engagement over time.

## Community strategy

The community strategy is based on making community interaction part of the product itself instead of treating community as just announcements and support.

Telegram is the strongest starting point because it is already a native environment for crypto users and communities. The Haus demonstrates that linked accounts and cashier-backed interactions can extend into Telegram, which makes it a strong channel for onboarding, updates, user support, and future reward or interaction loops.

As the product grows, the community layer can expand into tester groups, reward campaigns, challenges, leaderboard participation, and partner community activations. The goal is to build a community around usage, not just visibility. Users should be able to fund, spend, interact, receive rewards, and participate through connected product surfaces rather than only follow along passively.

Long term, community becomes part of retention and growth because The Haus is being designed to support active participation across app and social environments.

## Revenue & sustainability model

The Haus is designed to become sustainable by sitting at the balance, payment, and spend layer of interactive Avalanche products.

Revenue can come from cashier-level fees, module-level fees, partner usage, and future infrastructure access by applications that build on top of the system. The strength of the model is that it does not depend on one single front-end demo or one isolated feature. As more modules and integrations use the same cashier backend, the system becomes more useful and more sustainable without rebuilding the core infrastructure each time.

This creates a better long-term business model because the same underlying rails can support spend flows, rewards, community actions, app experiences, and future partner deployments. Over time, The Haus can generate value through direct product usage, shared infrastructure usage, and integrations for Avalanche builders who want smoother balance-backed application flows.

## Competitive landscape

One major alternative is the standard wallet-only dapp model where users approve or sign every important action directly through their wallet. That preserves ownership, but it creates too much friction for products that need repeated interaction. The Haus improves this by keeping a wallet-rooted foundation while adding a smoother cashier and spend layer.

Another alternative is single-purpose internal balance systems built for one app or one game only. Those can work for a narrow use case, but they are usually not reusable. The Haus is different because it is being built as modular infrastructure that can support multiple applications, auth paths, and interaction surfaces from one shared cashier model.

A third alternative is more closed or centralized account-balance systems that feel simpler but lose the wallet-backed nature and composability of web3. The Haus aims for the stronger middle ground by improving usability without giving up the value of wallet-linked ownership and broader web3 interoperability.

What makes users and builders choose The Haus is that it is not limited to one interface or one auth model. It already shows how the same cashier system can support wallet interaction, SIWE-style flows, Telegram-linked usage, and Google sign-in spend paths, and that flexibility gives it a stronger long-term product direction than narrower alternatives.
